# Backup Sight Field Notes

*Notes from setting up flip-up backup iron sights (BUIS) behind and in front of a red dot sight. General guidance only. Check your own rifle, follow your range's rules, and confirm everything with live fire.*

## Why carry backup irons at all

A red dot sight is quick to pick up, but it depends on a battery, an emitter and clean glass. Flip-up backup iron sights don't need any of those. They sit folded on the rail and stay out of the way until you need them.

## What "co-witness" means

Co-witness describes where the iron sights appear in the optic's window when they're flipped up:

- **Absolute co-witness:** the iron sight post lines up with the dot, in the middle of the window.
- **Lower 1/3 co-witness:** the irons appear in the bottom third of the window, leaving more of the view clear.

Which one you get depends on the optic's mount height and the iron sight height. Measure both, or check the manufacturers' published heights, rather than guessing.

## Checklist from the bench

1. Plan the rail space first. The rear sight has to fold flat without hitting the optic.
2. Mount the optic, then the rear and front flip-ups.
3. Flip the irons up and look through the optic. Note where the front post sits relative to the dot.
4. Zero the red dot at your chosen distance.
5. Zero the irons separately. Don't assume they match the dot.
6. Practise flipping the irons up by feel, without looking.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming co-witness without checking mount heights.
- Mounting the rear sight where it can't fold under or behind the optic.
- Never zeroing the irons, then discovering they're off the first time they're needed.
